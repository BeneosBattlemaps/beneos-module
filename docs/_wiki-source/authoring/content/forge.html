Your campaign already has loot. The +1 sword from session two, the heirloom amulet you wrote yourself, the strange ring the party pried off a dead wizard. The Forge folds all of it into the Beneos Origins systems, so your own items gain set bonuses, treasure-sense detectability, and a place in the wider mythology, without rebuilding anything.

What the Forge does

The Forge lives in the Items tab of the Codex and is a GM tool. It imprints any non-Beneos item with an Origin. Take a plain Foundry item, give it an Origin, and it becomes a full participant in the Beneos item ecosystem.

How to imprint an item

  1. Open the Forge in the Codex Items tab.
  2. Drag the item onto the anvil.
  3. Choose an Origin from the 19 archetypes.

That is the whole ritual. The item is imprinted.

What the item gains

Once imprinted, your item picks up everything a native Beneos item carries:

  • The Origin's name and lore, woven on so it reads as part of that archetype.
  • An Echo of Origin bonus, the opening set-tier reward.
  • Full attunement-tier mechanics, so it climbs toward Resonance and Perfect Harmony alongside other items of its Origin. See Origin Set Bonuses.
  • Detectability by the Item Radar, so an attuned player can sense it across the map. See The Item Radar.
  • Set-bonus tracking, so it shows up properly in the Beneos Sets tab and counts toward tiers.

Why this matters at the table

The Forge bridges your campaign and the Beneos systems. You do not have to choose between the loot you lovingly placed and the depth of Origins, set bonuses, and the radar. Imprint your existing treasures and they join the same living web: a homebrew amulet anchors a Vampiric set, your party's signature blade pings on a player's Sense Compass, and the artifact at the end of your campaign carries the weight of a real archetype.
