Magic items are more fun when they belong to something. A Beneos item is never just a name and a bonus: it carries an Origin, one of 19 magical archetypes that says where its power comes from, how it feels, and how it interacts with the rest of the party's gear. Origins are the backbone of the whole item system, feeding both set bonuses and the Item Radar.

The 19 Origins

Every Beneos item belongs to exactly one Origin. The full set:

What defines an item

Beyond its Origin, each item has two traits that shape play:

  • Rarity: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare, or Legendary. Rarity feeds directly into set-bonus math (see Origin Set Bonuses).
  • Named or Generic: a Named item is a specific, famous artifact with its own identity. A Generic item represents its Origin. The two count differently for set bonuses.

The Origins gallery

The Codex has an Items tab, and at its heart is the Origins gallery (1): 19 cards, one per Origin. Each card is a full briefing on that archetype.

Open a card and you get:

  • The Origin's lore, so you can place it in your world.
  • Its set-bonus tiers, the rewards for attuning multiple items of that Origin.
  • Sample items that belong to it.
  • An attunement simulator to preview the bonuses before you hand the loot out.

The item companion panel

Every item sheet gains a companion panel that shows the item's place in the system at a glance: the Origin emblem, its tier, whether it is named or generic, and its set contribution. Players and GMs read what an item is without digging.

What Origins power

Origins are not decoration. They drive three connected systems:

  • Set bonuses: attune several items of the same Origin to unlock tiered rewards. See Origin Set Bonuses.
  • The Item Radar: a GM tool that turns Origins into an in-fiction treasure sense. See The Item Radar.
  • The Forge: imprint your own existing loot with an Origin and fold it into all of the above. See The Forge.
