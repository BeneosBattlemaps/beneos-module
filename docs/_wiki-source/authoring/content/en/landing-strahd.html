Before you read: the landing page runs the moment you set it active. Everything below is for the GM who wants to work it, or rebuild it for another story. It is made for gothic-horror play and pairs with Curse of Strahd, but the pieces suit any dark campaign.

Like its infernal cousin, the Barovia landing page is a small set of scenes joined into one hub: a home page, the Barovia map, a wall of faces the party has met, a book with three tabs, and a table for the Tarokka reading. You reveal each part as the campaign reaches it. If you already read the Descent into Avernus page, most of the mechanics here will feel familiar; they share the same bones.

Landing page & navigation

This is the page your players land on when they log in, so keep it active by default at the start of a session. An animated Barovia menu frames the whole hub, and from here your players reach every scene you have opened to them.

The navigation bar is built the same way as every Beneos menu: an invisible clickable tile over a visible text label, run by Monks Active Tile Triggers. To open a destination for players, show its text tile and switch its invisible trigger on; the MATT deep dive walks through it. Each scene also carries its own ambient track, which you can change or silence under the scene's Ambience tab.

The Barovia map

A full map of Barovia, ready to sit at the heart of the campaign. Use it as the players' overview of the valley: where they are, where they have been, and where the mist still hides. It behaves like any Beneos map, so you can drop point-of-interest markers on it and link them to scenes and journals. See Point of Interest Teleporters for wiring those up.

The Characters page

Barovia is a small place with a long memory, and the party will meet its people again and again. This page keeps track of them, grouped by where they were met: the Village of Barovia, Durst Manor, the Vistani camp, Argynvostholt, Old Bonegrinder, Vallaki, Krezk, the ruins of Berez, the Amber Temple, the werewolf den, the Wizards of Wine, Castle Ravenloft.

Every portrait is a real actor. To reveal one, pick Token Controls, click the token, then right-click and Toggle Visibility State. Players see the face and, on hover, the name, but never the stat block. Add your own villagers the same way: drag the actor in, set its token's Display Name to "Hovered by Anyone," and show it when the party meets them.

The book: quests, notes, artefacts

The centrepiece is a book with three tabs, reached by two small sub-navigation points at the foot of the pages. Each tab is its own scene doing its own job.

Quests

The quest tab is the party's shared to-do list. The idea is simple: you jot the jobs here so your players always know what is open, without you repeating yourself. Type a quest's title into the text field with the text tool, and mark it off when it is done. Each checkbox is a tile you toggle: leave it empty, show the tick, or drop an X on it with the text tool for a job finished.

Player notes

The second tab is a plain journal, one page handed to each connected player, so everyone has their own space to scribble theories, names, and half-remembered warnings. It is the ordinary Foundry journal you already know, just parked somewhere the party will always find it.

Artefacts

The third tab tracks the three treasures that can turn the fight against Strahd: the Holy Symbol of Ravenkind, the Sun Sword, the Tome of Strahd, and the Icon of Ravenloft. Each sits hidden until it is earned. When the party gets their hands on one, pick its tile and Toggle Visibility State to reveal it on the page, so the group can watch their arsenal grow across the campaign.

The Tarokka reading

The last page is for the Tarokka reading, the card draw that seeds each Curse of Strahd game differently. It is a laying-out surface: a spread of five card slots your players fill with the cards their reading turned up.

One honest note on what we do and do not provide. We do not ship a Tarokka deck. We do not hold the rights to the official one, and there are far too many lovely fan decks for us to pick for you. So bring your own: run your reading however you like, then replace the five placeholder tiles with images of the cards you drew. From then on the whole group can see, at any point in the campaign, exactly which fates the cards dealt them.
