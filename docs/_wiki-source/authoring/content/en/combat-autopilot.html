Running a memorable monster means juggling whose turn it is, which round you are on, whether the creature already spent its big move, and whether it is making the entrance it deserves. The Combat Autopilot takes that bookkeeping off your plate. It lives on each creature's page in the Creature Codex and keeps a single fight on rails for the GM.

What it does

Tracks the round

A round counter you increment as combat moves forward. Many abilities scale or trigger by round, so the number stays front and center and you never second-guess where the fight stands.

Tracks ability usage

The creature's signature abilities are listed with cooldowns. Mark one used and the Autopilot tells you whether it is once-per-round or once-per-day and when it comes back. No more firing the dragon's breath two turns in a row, no more forgetting the recharge that would have turned the fight.

Nails the first appearance

The first time the creature shows up, the Autopilot offers a one-time first-appearance prompt, a cue for its debut. It helps you give the entrance weight, then steps out of the way.

How the state behaves

Tracking is scoped to the current session. Reload the world and it resets, so the next fight starts clean: fresh round count, every ability available. Nothing to clear by hand.

A GM aid, not an automation

The Autopilot is a co-pilot. It does not take actions for you: no rolling attacks, no moving the token, no spending resources. It tracks, reminds, and prompts. Every decision stays yours, by design, because the fun of running a monster is making the calls yourself.
