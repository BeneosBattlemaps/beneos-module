Beneos Battlemaps' standout feature is its interconnected maps. Instead of offering standalone maps, we link different locations. Many of our releases include multiple maps for a single location, all connected to an overview map. As a DM, you can seamlessly jump from one map to its corresponding scenery or maintain a bird's-eye view with the overview scene. The "Point of Interest Teleporter" module in Foundry facilitates this navigation. We've set everything up for you in advance, so this guide will explain the various interaction possibilities. If you're curious about the module itself and want to create your own links, there's a deep dive section below, but it's optional.

Note: All icons mentioned here are invisible to players. They're exclusively for DMs to aid in navigation without any technical know-how.

Navigation Using Point of Interest Teleporter

Maps have placed journal entries. Right-clicking offers two options for DMs:

  • "View Scene" switches only the DM to the target scene.
  • "Activate Scene" moves all active players to the target scene.

Make Journal Icons Visible On Your Scenes

If you can't see any journal icons on your maps, make sure to make them visible! To do this, go to the FoundryVTT toolbar on the left side, click on the banner icon, and then on the pinhead icon labeled "Toggle Notes Display". This will make all journal icons permanently visible.

  • Highlights the specific region or kingdom within Beneos' expansive fantasy world, Escalia. This map provides context, whether it's a kingdom or a specific region like Barovia.

  • A gateway back to the global map, offering jumps to continents or kingdoms. This map also utilizes the "Monks Active Tile Triggers" module. Remember to keep all modules active for a smooth experience!

  • Double-click to view an immersive shot of the current map. These are optional for DMs and can be used to highlight story-relevant aspects.

SCENERY

  • Takes you to an immersive, cinematic view of the current battlemap, ideal for role-playing moments.

BATTLEMAP

  • Switches to the tactical view of the current scenery, useful for combat or deeper exploration.

PLAYER INTRO

  • Offers specially produced video sequences for an immersive introduction to a scene (If available).

COMPASS

  • These icons, placed on the edges of battlemaps, indicate the direction of the target scene, providing a sense of direction and aiding in understanding the surroundings.

INFO

  • Some maps come with additional tips or information for the DM, always located in the top left corner.

Overlay Tiles

Foreground Layer If you've ever wondered how I achieve a sense of depth in the maps, where you can walk under trees or doors, it's thanks to Foreground Tiles. These are essentially normal tiles but are placed OVER, not under, the Tokens (or Actors, in Foundry language). This way, Foreground Layer Tiles beautifully convey depth because Tokens disappear behind walls and go under everything that logically lies above them in the map layout. Foreground Layers are part of the FoundryVTT Core, so no module is needed.

If you want to change or deactivate Foreground Layers, click on "Tile Controls" in your toolbar on the left side. By default, the Foreground Layer is hidden and can't be accidentally clicked. To change this, click on the house symbol, and all Foreground Layer Tiles become configurable like any other tile. Just be sure to click only on the tile that lies hierarchically on top. Tiles underneath (which can be up to 8 on some maps) can only be clicked if you move the top one aside. In most cases, the Foreground Layer Tile is exactly the same size as the actual map, so you don't have numerous small tiles scattered around the map.

When Foreground Layer Tiles are clickable via the house symbol, you can open the settings with a double-click, like any tile. You can also make overlay tiles invisible so players can't see them or use modules like Monks Active Tile Triggers to automate (If->Then) under certain conditions. More on MATT can be found in our DEEP DIVE: MONKS ACTIVE TILE TRIGGER. By the way, Foreground Layer Tiles can also be video files. If partially transparent, they can create cool waving tree effects (or similar). Of course, this also increases the performance requirements, so we use them very sparingly and thoughtfully.

The Foreground Tile Settings give you everything you need. Here, you can also "promote" a normal tile to a Foreground Layer Tile by activating the "Is Overhead?" checkbox. Just "Is Roof?" alone is not enough; this setting only affects how token sight range is calculated from a distance. The "Occlusion Mode" is also interesting.

  • None: The tile is ALWAYS displayed, which is right for most applications.
  • Fade: The entire tile fades out, useful if the Foreground Tile is a roof that fades out when Tokens are underneath it.
  • Radial: Essentially a cool feature because it constantly displays the Foreground Tile and only fades out a small area around the Token, or adjusts transparency based on the "Occlusion Alpha" slider. Unfortunately, the occlusion around the token is so small and unchangeable that it often looks like the Token is ON the Foreground Tile rather than under it.
  • Vision: This setting only fades out parts of the Foreground Layer Tile seen by a Token with active "Token Vision." That means players only really see the Foreground Layer Tile when it's in shadow. Not very intuitive.

How do I control the ambient and door sounds on the maps

It's quite simple. Importantly, you can't set how loud things are for your players; each player must adjust this for themselves. This is controlled in the "Playlist" tab in your top-right navigation bar.

  • The Playlist slider adjusts the volume of the DM's music, meaning all tracks that are played in the Playlist tab.

  • The most important slider is the "Ambient" slider. It controls the volume of intros, map sounds, and clicking sound effects on some maps with interactive features.

  • The "Interface" slider adjusts the volume of door sounds on the maps. If you find the sound of doors opening too loud, simply turn down the Interface slider.
