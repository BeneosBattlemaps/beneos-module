Before You Read

Here you'll find additional information that is not needed for using Beneos Battlemaps. All the sensible functions and settings have already been done for you in advance, and you can use everything immediately and intuitively. This deep dive is meant for those who want to add Fx Master functions to their own maps or are just curious about how the module works.

Why FX Master?

FX Master is a pure effects tool. Essentially, it's a library of image and particle effects that can be individually defined and saved for specific scenes with numerous settings. Especially the older releases, which were built with the older Unreal Engine and a less experienced me, benefit from falling leaves, sharper, more contrast-rich images, or fog and birds. FX Master gives me even more possibilities to shape individual scenes in post-production to the form I envisioned. You too can completely change the mood of the same map with it. With blue fireflies and a night filter, you turn a bright daytime forest into a Fey-Wild experience. Or, with the fog effect, it could also be the Swalich Wood from Curse of Strahd.

How Do I Use FX Master?

FX Master is incredibly easy to use and just as simple to access. Once you've activated FX Master in your world, you'll have a new icon in your toolbar on the left side - the magic wand. The most important settings are hidden behind "Particle Effects" and "Filter Effects," and are saved individually for each scene.

Particle Effects

As the name suggests, here you'll find all the effects that add something moving to the map. Each particle effect has settings like speed, density, color, size, etc. With Particle Effects, you can make birds fly over the map, leaves blow in the wind, or have rain/snow fall onto the map. The settings are self-explanatory. Play around a bit - it's always trial and error.

Filter Effects

Here you'll find everything that changes the map itself. You can make color adjustments as well as add lightning or a cool underwater effect to the map. There are many exciting settings, but essentially, you don't need to say anything about them. Just play around and see what you like.

Saving Settings

You can safe configurations and Applying Them to Other Maps Once you've configured a map the way you want it, i.e., selected and configured filter and particle effects, they are set ONLY for the current scene. To apply the effect to another scene, you need a macro that stores exactly the FX Master settings of that scene. Fortunately, this is done with one click. Using the floppy disk save icon, a new macro will be added to your macro list that contains all the changes to the current scene. I recommend giving the new macro a distinctive name with a right-click, as FX Master names all macros the same.

Drag the macro to your macro bar, and whenever you click this macro on a scene, all saved filters and particle effects will be applied. Then you can delete the macro.
