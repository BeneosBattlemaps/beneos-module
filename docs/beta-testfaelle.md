# Beneos Stream, what to try

Nine things, in order. None of them takes longer than a few minutes, and the
last two need a second person.

Work through them once, then play a normal session and forget the list. The
session is the real test; this list only makes sure nothing is skipped by
accident.

For each one, tell us what you saw. "Worked" is a useful answer. "Worked, but
the first three seconds were a still picture" is a better one.

---

## 1. Install a release

Open the Cloud window and install any battlemap release.

**Watch for:** how long it takes, and how much it adds to your Foundry data
folder. Both should be far smaller than you are used to.

## 2. Open a scene

Open one of the scenes you just installed.

**Watch for:** does the map move right away, or do you see a still picture
first? If you see a still picture, how long before the video takes over?

## 3. Open the same scene again

Close it, open it again.

**Watch for:** this one should be faster than the first time. If it is not,
that is worth knowing.

## 4. Look at the storage bar

In the Cloud window, above the map list, there is a bar. Click it.

**Watch for:** does the explanation that opens make sense to you? This text is
new and we would rather hear it is confusing now than after release.

## 5. Keep one scene offline

Right-click a scene in the sidebar and choose "Keep offline". Wait for it to
finish.

**Watch for:** does the storage bar move? Does the scene say somewhere that it
is now kept?

## 6. Pull the plug

Turn off your internet. Open the scene you kept offline, then open one you did
not.

**Watch for:** the kept one should play with video and sound. The other one
should still open, with a still picture instead of video, and tokens, walls
and lighting should all work. Neither should fail with an error.

Turn your internet back on afterwards and open both again.

## 7. Install a creature, an item and a spell

These are not streamed. They install the old way.

**Watch for:** anything that behaves differently from what you are used to.
This step exists to catch what we broke, not what we built.

## 8. Play with a player

Have someone join your world and move a token around on a streamed map.

**Watch for:** does the map move for them too? Ask them where they are,
roughly, and whether it felt slower than your usual games.

## 9. Play a real session

The one that matters. Two or three hours, several scenes, the normal mess.

**Watch for:** anything that pulled you out of the game. A pause, a black
rectangle, a sound that came late, a scene that took too long. Note the scene
name and roughly the time, that is enough for us to find it.

---

## How to tell us

Write it however you like. A list, a voice note, a running commentary, all
fine.

The one thing we ask: when something looks broken, give us the **scene name**
and **roughly when** it happened. The module reports delivery failures by
itself, and those two details let us match your sentence to its report.
